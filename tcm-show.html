A live performance is not one system. It is many systems that have to move together. Coordinating music, technology, and execution.

02

The System

Musicians + Playback + FOH + IEM + Stage + Vocals + Instruments → One Live Performance

The complexity of the live environment requires precise orchestration. The stage was divided into discrete zones for drums, guitars, bass, keyboards, ethnic instruments, playback, and vocals. Each of these zones fed into two Midas M32 digital mixers.

Simultaneously, an 18-set wireless IEM system and a complex playback rig ran concurrently. Success depended on ensuring these individual components operated as a unified, seamless technical architecture.

03

My Contribution

My role sat at the intersection of music preparation and technical execution. I was not the overall show director or the audio engineer—my focus was building the musical assets and coordinating their integration into the technical environment.

What I Built

  • Ableton backing tracks for ~50% of the setlist
  • MuseScore sheet music for ~50% of the setlist
  • The master setlist and timing documentation

What I Coordinated

  • Live performance on stage
  • Playback system cues and technical requirements
  • Connecting musical needs to the audio engineering team

Crucially, the playback system required distinct logic: the Click and Guide channels were routed exclusively to the band's In-Ear Monitors (IEM), bypassing Front of House entirely. Only the Backing L/R stems were sent to both FOH and IEMs.

Coordination

The complexity was not just technical—it was operational. To ensure all systems moved together, the master setlist acted as the central control layer.
